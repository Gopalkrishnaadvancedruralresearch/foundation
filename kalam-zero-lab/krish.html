Why does QAOA take ~120 ms while Grover Search takes only ~12 μs?

Students frequently ask: "If both are quantum algorithms, why is QAOA 10,000 times slower than Grover?" The answer is the architectural difference between a Pure Quantum Algorithm and a Hybrid Variational Algorithm:

Grover Search (~12 μs) — Pure Quantum

• Direct Unitary Execution: Once the oracle and diffusion operators are prepared, the circuit runs straight through the quantum hardware without stopping.
• Single Hardware Pass: The entire search space across 700 verses (218 states) is evaluated in ≈ π/4√N iterations in a few microsecond coherence bursts.

QAOA (~120 ms) — Hybrid Variational Loop

• Classical-Quantum Ping-Pong: QAOA does not run just once. A classical CPU calculates angle parameters (γ, β), sends them to the QPU over a bus, reads the measurement, updates parameters via gradient descent, and repeats for 50 to 200 cycles.
• Shot Noise Sampling: In each cycle, the quantum circuit must be measured 1,000 to 10,000 times ("shots") to calculate the statistical expectation value E(γ, β). Total time is driven by classical I/O and shot accumulation!

The Three Computational Paradigms

1. CLASSICAL AI

Runs strictly on Von Neumann silicon hardware (CPUs & GPU Tensor Cores). Processes text as discrete binary subwords, matrices, and continuous neural weights. Bound by memory bandwidth and quadratic attention ceilings.

2. HYBRID AI (VARIATIONAL)

A collaborative tandem of classical CPU optimizers and parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs). Uses the quantum coprocessor for state evaluation while the classical host handles loss calculations. Bound by parameter convergence iterations and shot count.

3. QUANTUM AI (PURE)

Direct coherent quantum operations in 2ⁿ-dimensional Hilbert state space. Exploits superposition, entanglement, and interference to solve algebraic and search problems with polynomial or exponential speedups.
