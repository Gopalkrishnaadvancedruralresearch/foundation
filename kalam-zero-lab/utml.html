How to Use, Understanding & Help

This virtual lab simulates a closed-loop thermal process controlled by a Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) algorithm. The objective is to tune the controller parameters ($K_p$, $K_i$, $K_d$) so that the Process Variable (PV) reaches and maintains the Setpoint (SP) quickly and accurately.

Core Controls Overview:

  • Target Temp (SP): Set the desired operational temperature in °C.
  • Gains ($K_p, K_i, K_d$): Adjust the controller sensitivity to error, accumulated error over time, and rate of error change.
  • Cooling Shock Button: Simulates a sudden external thermal load disturbance to test the controller's disturbance rejection capability.
  • Sensor Noise Slider: Adds simulated Gaussian measurement noise to replicate real-world thermocouple or RTD signal degradation.

Step-by-Step PID Tuning Guide

Follow this standard manual tuning procedure (Ziegler-Nichols / Heuristic approach) to optimize your thermal system control:

  1. Set All Gains to Zero: Move $K_p$, $K_i$, and $K_d$ sliders down to $0.0$. Turn off advanced toggles.
  2. Increase Proportional Gain ($K_p$): Slowly increase $K_p$ until the system responds to a setpoint change. Notice that with only $K_p$, the temperature will stabilize slightly below the target due to steady-state error.
  3. Introduce Integral Gain ($K_i$): Gradually increase $K_i$ to eliminate steady-state error. Watch how the integral term drives the error to zero. Warning: Setting $K_i$ too high will cause large oscillations and overshoot.
  4. Add Derivative Gain ($K_d$): Increase $Kd$ to add damping. This reduces overshoot and smooths out rapid temperature transitions.
  5. Enable Advanced Features: Enable Anti-Windup to prevent actuator saturation windup, and turn on the Derivative Low-Pass Filter if you increase sensor noise.

Why This Interactive Lab is Valuable for Engineering Students

Physical PID training rigs (like motor workstations, flow rigs, or thermal chambers) cost lakhs of rupees, require space, and are prone to hardware failure. This web-based platform delivers several advantages:

  • Zero Cost & Equal Access: Replaces physical setups costing ₹5L–₹15L with a browser platform accessible on budget devices.
  • Real-Time Visualization: Instantly displays $P$, $I$, and $D$ component contributions alongside integrated error metrics ($IAE$ and $ITAE$).
  • Safe Failure Exploration: Allows students to observe actuator saturation, integrator windup, and noise amplification without risk of thermal thermal runaway or hardware damage.
  • Practical Engineering Features: Exposes students to industry-standard techniques such as Anti-Windup clamping, Derivative-on-Measurement filtering, and Feedforward action.

Target Engineering Streams

This lab provides practical experience for students across several engineering departments:

Electrical & Electronics Engineering (EEE)

Focuses on feedback loops, steady-state error analysis, stability margins, and power control signals.

Instrumentation & Control Engineering (ICE)

Essential for studying process dynamics, sensor noise filtering, auto-tuning methods, and quantitative performance indices (IAE/ITAE).

Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering

Applies directly to thermal management systems, engine temperature regulation, HVAC controls, and environmental chambers.

Chemical Engineering

Useful for studying CSTR temperature control, heat exchangers, distillation column control, and lag time dynamics.

Robotics & Mechatronics

Applies closed-loop control theory to motor torque control, joint positioning, and sensor filtering strategies.
