Historical Scientific Priority • Metamaterial SHM

Pioneered by Dr. Annamdas Venu Gopal Madhav & Prof. Soh Chee Kiong (NTU Singapore)

Prior to the seminal research conducted by Annamdas and Soh at Nanyang Technological University, electromagnetic metamaterials and split-ring resonators (SRRs) were confined strictly to electromagnetic cloaking, telecommunication filter circuits, and radar cross-section absorbers.

In their landmark IWSHM 2015 (10th International Workshop on Structural Health Monitoring, Stanford University) presentation, Annamdas and Soh proved for the first time in global scientific history that a passive microwave circular metamaterial could track structural deflection, strain, and load severity across the 3 to 8 GHz band through localized surface plasmon (LSP) resonance shifts near 6.035 GHz in both bonded contact and non-contact near-field configurations.

Governing Physics & Quantum Mathematical Formulations

Bridging classical electromagnetic metamaterial theory with quantum neural network inversion.

1. Annamdas LSP Circuit Resonance

The circular passive metamaterial behaves as an effective $L_{\text{eff}} - C_{\text{eff}}$ resonator whose resonant frequency $f_0$ shifts as mechanical strain perturbs the substrate dielectric permittivity $\varepsilon_{\text{eff}}$:

$$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L_{\text{eff}} \cdot C_{\text{eff}}(\sigma_{xx}, d_{\text{gap}})}}$$

Evanescent near-field coupling decays exponentially with standoff: $\Delta\varepsilon_{\text{eff}} \propto \exp(-d_{\text{gap}}/\delta_p)$, where $\delta_p$ is the reactive penetration depth.

2. Annamdas RMSD Damage Index

Damage severity is quantified across the transmission band using the Root Mean Square Deviation between the pristine baseline spectrum and the in-situ spectrum:

$$\text{RMSD} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \left[\text{Re}(S_{21}^{\text{load}}(f_i)) - \text{Re}(S_{21}^{\text{base}}(f_i))\right]^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N \left[\text{Re}(S_{21}^{\text{base}}(f_i))\right]^2}} \times 100\%$$

Allows instant classification: $\text{RMSD} \le 2\%$ Pristine, $2-10\%$ Incipient Damage, and $>15\%$ Severe Fracture.

3. Sub-THz Photoelastic Birefringence

Sub-millimeter G-band waves experience stress-induced optical anisotropy, enabling direct reconstruction of principal stress differences via phase delay:

$$\Delta n = n_x - n_y = C_{\text{photo}}(\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy})$$

Rayleigh scattering cross-section follows $\sigma_{\text{RCS}} \propto a^6 / \lambda_0^4$, giving high sensitivity to micro-cracks ($a < 150\,\mu\text{m}$).

4. Ka/V Guided Surface Lamb Waves

Planar metamaterial elements couple into Lamb and Rayleigh surface modes across 26.5–75 GHz. Delaminations and adhesive bondline defects scatter the forward wave:

$$k_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{eff}}\mu_{\text{eff}}} - j\alpha_{\text{delam}}$$

Enables high-resolution composite skin and adhesive bondline integrity scanning in aerospace components.

5. L/S Volumetric Civil Penetration

Lower microwave frequencies (1–3 GHz) penetrate deeply into heterogeneous lossy materials like reinforced concrete without severe attenuation:

$$\alpha = \omega \sqrt{\frac{\mu \varepsilon'}{2}\left[\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\varepsilon''}{\varepsilon'}\right)^2} - 1\right]}$$

Allows detection of internal voids, deep rebar corrosion, and moisture ingress behind concrete covers.

6. Quantum Parameter-Shift Inversion

Parameterized Quantum Circuits evaluate exact analytical gradients directly on quantum hardware without finite-difference errors:

$$\frac{\partial \langle \hat{O} \rangle}{\partial \theta_j} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\langle \hat{O} \rangle_{\theta_j + \frac{\pi}{2}} - \langle \hat{O} \rangle_{\theta_j - \frac{\pi}{2}}\right]$$

Eliminates non-uniqueness and local traps when inverting multi-frequency scattering data to reconstruct $[\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \tau_{xy}]$.

USER MANUAL • SCIENTIFIC GROUNDING & EXPERIMENTS

1. Scientific Justification for the 4 Strategic Bands

Structural damage operates on a hierarchical multi-scale continuum: macroscopic bending, bondline disbonds, and incipient micro-cracks. No single frequency can detect all damage regimes due to wavelength-to-flaw coupling ($\lambda \sim a$).

L/S-Band (1–3 GHz, $\lambda \approx 100-300\text{ mm}$): Penetrates deeply through thick, lossy heterogeneous concrete covers to detect sub-surface rebar corrosion and void formation without excessive scattering attenuation.
Annamdas & Soh Band (3–8 GHz, $\lambda \approx 37.5-100\text{ mm}$): Historical milestone established at IWSHM 2015 (Stanford). Tracks macroscopic flexure, strain, and joint integrity via contact and non-contact localized surface plasmons near 6.035 GHz.
Ka/V-Band (26.5–75 GHz, $\lambda \approx 4-11.3\text{ mm}$): Optimized for planar metasurface surface wave launching, resolving CFRP composite delaminations, and inspecting aerospace adhesive bondlines.
Sub-THz G-Band (110–300 GHz, $\lambda \approx 1-2.7\text{ mm}$): Sub-millimeter wavelengths interact directly with microscopic cracks ($<150\,\mu\text{m}$) via Rayleigh backscattering, while photoelastic birefringence visualizes internal stress concentration contours.
