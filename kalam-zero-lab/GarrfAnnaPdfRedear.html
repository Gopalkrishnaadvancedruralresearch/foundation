📖 Prof. GARRF-Anna – Guide, Help and Disclaimers

1. What this is

Prof. GARRF-Anna turns a research paper, lecture notes or a book chapter (PDF or Word) into a classroom. The document is on the left and the professor’s green board is on the right. You choose what to learn, and the professor teaches it one small piece at a time, at your pace.

2. Quick start

  • Click Upload PDF / Word and choose your file (.pdf or .docx).
  • Click a section button under the document toolbar (for example Abstract or Introduction), or click any heading, paragraph, figure, table or equation in the document. You can also select any text with the mouse.
  • The first piece appears on the board. Press Continue to add the next piece below it. Nothing advances on its own, so take your time to read and understand.
  • Previous removes the last piece, Repeat reads the newest piece again, Read Aloud / Pause / Stop control the voice, and Generate Compact Summary gives a short summary of what you selected.
  • Use Level (Undergraduate, Postgraduate, PhD, Faculty), Accent, Voice and Speed in the strip above the board.
  • Type or speak a question in Ask Prof. GARRF-Anna below the board.

3. What it does

  • Two equal screens (50:50): document on the left, board on the right, both the same height.
  • Section navigation: detects headings and shows them as buttons. Figure and table buttons appear when found.
  • Click to teach: the clicked location is highlighted and the professor stands beside it.
  • Step-by-step board: each Continue appends the next piece, so the whole lesson stays on the board. A chalk marks the newest line.
  • Equations: clicking an equation, or its number such as (1), selects the whole equation. It is shown on the board exactly as printed. The professor says what the equation relates and then explains each symbol (for example ν, nu) using the sentence in the document that defines it, found anywhere in the file. She does not read the equation letter by letter.
  • Figures and tables: explained in words from the caption and the paper’s own sentences about them.
  • Read aloud with word highlighting for the newest piece only. The whole document is never read automatically.
  • Footnote and header filtering: footnotes, copyright lines, page numbers and repeated headers or footers are skipped.
  • Privacy: your file is processed in your browser and is not stored on the cloud.

4. Basic mode and AI mode

  • The badge at the top right of the board panel shows the mode.
  • Basic mode (no AI): lessons are the document’s own sentences in small pieces; nothing is invented, but there is no genuine reasoning. Summaries pick important sentences from the text. Questions are answered by keyword matching.
  • AI mode: connect an AI service with the ⚙ button. It gives real explanations, simple-English rewording, derivations, typeset LaTeX equations, figure and table interpretation, and research advice (extensions, M.E. or PhD project ideas), clearly marked as the professor’s suggestions.
  • For the administrator: the ⚙ button takes the address of your own secure server (proxy) that holds the AI key. Never put an AI key inside this HTML file. The expected request and reply format is described in the comments at the top of the page’s script.

5. Will it work for a textbook?

Partly. It opens and navigates a textbook, but the quality of teaching depends on the AI service you connect.

  • Works well: chapter-sized text PDFs or .docx files with clear headings, with section buttons, click-to-teach, step-by-step lessons, and symbol definitions from across the chapter.
  • Scanned books: a scan has no selectable text, so nothing can be read. There is no OCR.
  • Very large files: a 400-page PDF is analysed page by page when uploaded and can be slow or freeze for a while. Use one chapter at a time.
  • Complex layouts: sidebars, boxed examples, margin notes, worked solutions and multi-column pages can be split or ordered wrongly. Headings are guessed from font sizes, so some may be missed or falsely detected.
  • Questions about the whole book: when AI is connected, a typed question sends only the first 40,000 characters of the file, so questions about later chapters may lack context.

6. Equations in detail

  • In Basic mode the equation is shown as an exact picture of the printed page, because reading math symbols out of a PDF is not reliable.
  • A best-effort LaTeX version is offered under ✎ Edit LaTeX. It may be wrong, especially for matrices and fractions. Correct it there and the typeset version appears on the board.
  • With an AI service that can read images, the equation is transcribed to LaTeX and typeset on the board.
  • Equations written with Word’s equation editor are dropped when a .docx file is opened.
  • If a symbol’s definition is not written in the document’s text (or its characters are encoded oddly), the professor says it is not defined nearby.

7. Figures and tables

  • The professor cannot look inside a figure or table image. She explains from the caption and from the sentences in the paper that mention it. Always check against the original.

8. Tips for best results

  • Use text-based PDFs (you can select text in them) or .docx files.
  • Upload one paper or one chapter at a time.
  • Click the specific paragraph, equation or caption you are studying rather than the whole document.
  • Choose your Level before starting; changing it re-teaches the current selection.
  • Use Chrome or Edge for the best voice, voice input and PDF support. Firefox does not support voice input.
  • For an Indian English voice, install an English (India) voice in your device’s language settings.
  • Always verify important facts, numbers and equations against the original document.

9. Possible future improvements

These are suggestions, not promises:

  • A chapter or page-range picker, and faster loading for big books.
  • Sending only the most relevant passages to the AI when answering questions about a whole book.
  • OCR for scanned pages and reading Word equations.
  • Reading figure images and table cells with an image-capable AI.

10. Limitations (summary)

  • No OCR; scanned documents will not work. Old .doc files are not supported (save as .docx).
  • Structure detection is automatic and imperfect; always check against the document.
  • Basic mode does not reason, derive, compare or advise.
  • Voice quality depends on the voices installed on your device. Voice input needs an internet connection.
  • The page loads PDF, Word and maths libraries from the internet when it first opens. After it has loaded, your file stays in your browser and you can go offline.

11. Privacy and access

  • Your files are processed in your browser and are not stored on the cloud. You can safely go offline once the page has loaded.
  • If you connect an AI service, the text of the section or question you select is sent to that service. Use only a service you trust.
  • The password screen is a basic access gate inside the page. It keeps casual visitors out, but anyone who can read the page’s source code can bypass it. For strong protection, also protect the folder on the web server (for example server password protection or an access-control service).

12. Troubleshooting

  • Page does not update after a new version: press Ctrl+F5 for a hard refresh.
  • “Could not read this file”: the file may be scanned, protected or damaged. Press F12, open the Console tab and note the first red error.
  • No sound: check device volume and that the tab is not muted, press 🔈 Test voice, or try Chrome or Edge.
  • Yellow “fake worker” warning: harmless; large files may just load a little slower.
  • Logo missing: place logo.jpeg in the same folder as this HTML file.
