SECTION 1

1. Theoretical Foundations & Model Classification

Guided Stress Waves in Bounded Plates:
Guided waves satisfy traction-free boundary conditions for elastodynamic Navier equations across plate geometries. In this module, the fundamental symmetric and antisymmetric Lamb-wave modes are first studied theoretically; the actuator and sensor/PZT arrangement is introduced later in the specimen setup section.

1.1 Rayleigh-Lamb Characteristic Equations

For an isotropic elastic plate of total thickness $d$ (half-thickness $h = d/2$), density $\rho$, and bulk wave speeds $c_L, c_T$, the characteristic relations are:

$$\text{Symmetric Mode }(S): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} + \frac{4k^2 pq}{(k^2 - q^2)^2} = 0$$
$$\text{Antisymmetric Mode }(A): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} + \frac{(k^2 - q^2)^2}{4k^2 pq} = 0$$

where $p^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_L^2} - k^2$ and $q^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_T^2} - k^2$. Phase velocity is defined as $c_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$, and wavepacket envelope propagation follows the group velocity $c_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk}$.

Symmetric ($S_0$) Extensional Mode

In-plane displacement dominant; faster propagation velocity; symmetric about neutral plane $y=0$.

Antisymmetric ($A_0$) Flexural Mode

Out-of-plane bending dominant; slower, dispersive velocity; sensitive to surface-breaking defects.

3.1 Actuator & Sensor Introduction — Read This First
The specimen now introduces the physical measurement arrangement used in this SHM simulation. A surface-bonded piezoelectric wafer (PZT) is used as an actuator to launch guided Lamb waves, while receiving PZT elements are used as sensors to detect the propagating wave packet and damage-scattered response. The actuator and sensors are placed at defined coordinates on the plate. Only after understanding this arrangement should you change the coordinates or investigate damage.

6. Inverse Problem Formulation & ARJUN Assistant

Inverse Formulation & Non-Linear Least-Squares:
For actuator $\mathbf{x}_A$ and sensor $\mathbf{x}_{Si}$, the objective function minimized across $N$ paths is:

$$\min_{x_D, y_D} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left( \sqrt{(x_D - x_A)^2 + (y_D - y_A)^2} + \sqrt{(x_{Si} - x_D)^2 + (y_{Si} - y_D)^2} - c_g \cdot ToF_{s,i} \right)^2$$

The numerical optimization iteratively identifies coordinates within specimen boundaries $[0, L] \times [0, W]$. The reconstructed coordinate is shown on the 2D visualization plane.

SECTION 7

7. M.E./M.Tech Experimental Tasks, Validation & Viva

  1. Dispersion: vary $f\cdot d$ and plot $c_p$ and $c_g$ for S0 and A0. Explain dispersion and mode selection.
  2. ToF: calculate direct-path ToF and percentage error between theoretical and observed values.
  3. Noise sensitivity: repeat localization at 40, 30, 20 and 10 dB SNR and compare residual error.
  4. Damage sensitivity: vary crack length and angle; relate the synthetic damage index and scattered amplitude to the imposed parameters.
  5. Inverse localization: hide the true coordinates conceptually, use multi-sensor ToF, and evaluate reconstructed position and localization error.
Assessment requirement: Students should report equations, input parameters, dispersion plots, A-scans, ToF calculations, SNR, reconstructed coordinates, residuals, and a short discussion of numerical limitations.
