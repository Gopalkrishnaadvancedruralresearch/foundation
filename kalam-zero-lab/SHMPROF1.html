SECTION 1

1. Theory & Mathematical Foundations

Guided Stress Waves in Bounded Elastic Media:
Guided waves represent traction-free boundary condition solutions to elastodynamic Navier equations within plate geometries. Actuation is achieved by surface-bonded Lead Zirconate Titanate ($PZT$) transducers converting electrical potential fields into localized mechanical surface traction via the transverse piezoelectric strain coefficient ($d_{31}$).

1.1 Dispersive Lamb Modes: Symmetric ($S_0$) vs. Antisymmetric ($A_0$)

For an isotropic elastic plate of total thickness $d = 2h$, density $\rho$, and Lamé parameters $\lambda, \mu$, Lamb wave propagation is governed by the transcendental characteristic relations:

$$\text{Symmetric Mode }(S): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} = -\frac{4k^2 pq}{(k^2 - q^2)^2}$$
$$\text{Antisymmetric Mode }(A): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} = -\frac{(k^2 - q^2)^2}{4k^2 pq}$$

where the wavenumber parameters $p$ and $q$ depend upon the angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$, bulk longitudinal wave velocity $c_L = \sqrt{(\lambda + 2\mu)/\rho}$, and bulk shear wave velocity $c_T = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}$:

$$p^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_L^2} - k^2, \qquad q^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_T^2} - k^2$$

Symmetric ($S_0$) Extensional Mode

In-plane displacement dominant; symmetric about neutral plane $y=0$.

Antisymmetric ($A_0$) Flexural Mode

Out-of-plane bending dominant; surfaces translate in phase along thickness coordinate.

1.2 Dispersion, Phase Velocity ($c_p$) and Group Velocity ($c_g$)

Because wavenumbers $k(\omega)$ are non-linear functions of frequency, individual monochromatic wavefronts travel at the phase velocity $c_p = \omega / k$, whereas the actual mechanical packet energy propagates at the group velocity $c_g$:

$$c_g = \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k} = c_p + k \frac{\partial c_p}{\partial k} = c_p \left(1 - \frac{\lambda_w}{c_p}\frac{\partial c_p}{\partial \lambda_w}\right)$$
⚠️ Postgraduate Guidance: In all experimental Time-of-Flight ($ToF$) diagnostics and defect localization, packet arrival times are governed exclusively by group velocity $c_g$, not phase velocity $c_p$.

1.3 Direct & Scattered Time-of-Flight ($ToF$) Kinematics

Given an actuator $A(X_A, Y_A)$ and sensor $S(X_S, Y_S)$, direct line-of-sight propagation distance $d_{\text{direct}}$ and theoretical arrival time $ToF_{\text{direct}}$ are expressed as:

$$d_{\text{direct}} = \|\mathbf{x}_S - \mathbf{x}_A\|_2 = \sqrt{(X_S - X_A)^2 + (Y_S - Y_A)^2}$$
$$ToF_{\text{direct}} = \frac{d_{\text{direct}}}{c_g}$$

When a structural discontinuity is located at $D(X_D, Y_D)$, the acoustic perturbation creates a scattered wavepath $d_{\text{scatter}}$ with arrival time $ToF_{\text{scatter}}$ and travel time difference $\Delta ToF$:

$$d_{\text{scatter}} = d_{AD} + d_{DS} = \sqrt{(X_D - X_A)^2 + (Y_D - Y_A)^2} + \sqrt{(X_S - X_D)^2 + (Y_S - Y_D)^2}$$
$$ToF_{\text{scatter}} = \frac{d_{AD} + d_{DS}}{c_g}, \qquad \Delta ToF = ToF_{\text{scatter}} - ToF_{\text{direct}} = \frac{d_{\text{scatter}} - d_{\text{direct}}}{c_g}$$
