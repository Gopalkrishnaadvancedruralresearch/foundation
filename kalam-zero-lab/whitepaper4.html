Chapter 1: Elastodynamics, Dispersion & Lamb Waves

Guided elastic waves in plates represent bounded solutions to Navier's dynamic equilibrium equations[cite: 5]:

$$(\lambda + \mu)\nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}) + \mu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} = \rho \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{u}}{\partial t^2}$$

Enforcing traction-free boundaries ($\sigma_{yy} = 0, \tau_{xy} = 0$) at plate surfaces $y = \pm h$ (thickness $d = 2h$) yields the transcendental Rayleigh–Lamb relations[cite: 1, 5]:

$$\text{Symmetric Mode }(S): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} + \frac{4k^2 pq}{(k^2 - q^2)^2} = 0$$
$$\text{Antisymmetric Mode }(A): \quad \frac{\tan(qh)}{\tan(ph)} + \frac{(k^2 - q^2)^2}{4k^2 pq} = 0$$

where $p^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_L^2} - k^2$ and $q^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c_T^2} - k^2$[cite: 1, 5]. Bulk longitudinal speed is $c_L = \sqrt{(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho}$ and shear speed is $c_T = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}$[cite: 1, 5].

Phase Velocity ($c_p$) vs. Group Velocity ($c_g$):

In dispersive media, monochromatic wave crests move at phase velocity $c_p = \omega/k$, whereas wavepacket envelope energy propagates at group velocity[cite: 1, 5]:

$$c_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = c_p + k \frac{dc_p}{dk} = c_p \left(1 - \frac{\lambda_w}{c_p}\frac{dc_p}{d\lambda_w}\right)$$

Experimental Rule: Wavepacket envelope arrivals and Time-of-Flight (ToF) damage localization are governed by group velocity $c_g$[cite: 1, 5].

ToF Kinematics:

$$d_{\text{direct}} = \|\mathbf{x}_S - \mathbf{x}_A\|_2, \quad \text{ToF}_{\text{direct}} = \frac{d_{\text{direct}}}{c_g}$$ $$d_{\text{scatter}} = d_{AD} + d_{DS}, \quad \text{ToF}_{\text{scatter}} = \frac{d_{AD} + d_{DS}}{c_g}$$ $$\Delta \text{ToF} = \text{ToF}_{\text{scatter}} - \text{ToF}_{\text{direct}} = \frac{d_{\text{scatter}} - d_{\text{direct}}}{c_g}$$

Chapter 3: Electromechanical Impedance & Annamdas-Soh Formulations

The Electromechanical Impedance (EMI) technique uses the converse and direct piezoelectric effects simultaneously via high-frequency structural interrogation[cite: 1, 2].

1D Formulation (Liang et al.):

$$Y(\omega) = j\omega \frac{w_a \ell_a}{h_a} \left[ \bar{\varepsilon}_{33}^T - d_{31}^2 \bar{Y}^E + \left( \frac{Z_a}{Z_s + Z_a} \right) d_{31}^2 \bar{Y}^E \left( \frac{\tan(\kappa \ell_a)}{\kappa \ell_a} \right) \right]$$

Multi-Directional Formulations by Annamdas and Soh:

Real-world structures exhibit multi-axial dynamic stress states. Annamdas and Soh developed three-dimensional, multi-directional elastodynamic formulations for both surface-bonded and embedded PZT transducers:

$$Y(\omega) = j\omega \frac{w_a \ell_a}{h_a} \left[ \bar{\varepsilon}_{33}^T - \frac{2 d_{31}^2 \bar{Y}^E}{1 - \nu} + \frac{d_{31}^2 \bar{Y}^E}{1 - \nu} \left( \frac{Z_{a,x} Z_{a,y}}{Z_{s,x} Z_{s,y} - Z_{a,x} Z_{a,y}} \right) \psi_{2D}(\kappa_x \ell_a, \kappa_y w_a) \right]$$

Their work also established closed-form solutions for embedded PZT transducers in massive concrete and composite structures, accounting for spherical wave dissipation and normal/shear coupling impedances ($Z_{s,zz}, Z_{s,xz}, Z_{s,yz}$).

Damage Quantification Metric (RMSD):

$$\text{RMSD} (\%) = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} [G(\omega_i) - G_0(\omega_i)]^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{M} [G_0(\omega_i)]^2}} \times 100$$

Chapter 5: Thermal Compensation & SHMPROF4 Lab

Lab Suite 4: 3D Multi-Structural EMI & Thermal Compensation Lab (Prof. Ananth)[cite: 1, 2]

Ambient temperature variations introduce false-positive damage indicators by altering dielectric permittivity ($\varepsilon_{33}^T$) and softening structural stiffness ($E$), which shifts baseline resonance peaks downward[cite: 1, 2].

Effective Frequency Shift (EFS) Compensation:

To isolate true structural damage, the damaged signature is shifted across frequency increments $\Delta \omega$ to maximize cross-correlation with baseline $G_0$[cite: 1, 2]:

$$CC(\Delta \omega) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^M [G_0(\omega_i) - \bar{G}_0][G(\omega_i + \Delta \omega) - \bar{G}]}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^M [G_0(\omega_i) - \bar{G}_0]^2 \sum_{i=1}^M [G(\omega_i + \Delta \omega) - \bar{G}]^2}}$$

Compensated Damage Index:

$$\text{RMSD}_{\text{comp}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^M [G(\omega_i + \Delta \omega^*) - G_0(\omega_i)]^2}{\sum_{i=1}^M [G_0(\omega_i)]^2}} \times 100$$
