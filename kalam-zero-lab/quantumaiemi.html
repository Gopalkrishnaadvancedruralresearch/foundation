Fundamental Theory: PZT Electromechanical Admittance

The electromechanical impedance (EMI) technique utilizes the converse and direct piezoelectric effects of a surface-bonded PZT patch. The coupled 1D electromechanical admittance equation is governed by:

\(Y(\omega) = G(\omega) + jB(\omega) = 2\omega j \frac{w_p l_p}{h_p} \left[ \overline{\varepsilon}_{33}^T - \frac{Z_s(\omega)}{Z_s(\omega) + Z_a(\omega)} d_{31}^2 \overline{Y}^E \right]\)
  • \(G(\omega)\) (Conductance): Real part; sensitive to mechanical damping and structural stiffness degradation.
  • \(B(\omega)\) (Susceptance): Imaginary part; primarily dominated by PZT dielectric capacitance (\(\omega C\)).
  • \(Z_a(\omega)\) and \(Z_s(\omega)\): Mechanical impedance of the PZT transducer and the host structure, respectively.
  • Damage Coupling: Any local crack, corrosion, or loss of bolt pre-load directly modifies \(Z_s(\omega)\), triggering measurable peak shifts in \(G(\omega)\).

Waveform Excitation Physics

1. Single Sine: Steady-state harmonic voltage response. Single-tone signals often fail to expose internal damage because subtle phase delays resemble boundary impedance reflections.
2. Swept Sine (Core EMI - Option 2): High-frequency continuous frequency sweep (20 kHz to 150 kHz). Captures multiple mechanical modal resonances simultaneously via coupled Conductance (\(G\)) and Susceptance (\(B\)).
3. Step Function: Heaviside step input. Injects sharp elastodynamic stress waves into the host substrate, allowing students to observe acoustic reflection echoes returning from crack boundaries.
4. Triangle Wave: Constant slew rate excitation. Demonstrates contact acoustic non-linearities, breathing crack clapping mechanisms, and structural hysteresis.
