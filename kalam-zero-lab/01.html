Experiment 01 — Ohm's Law

Welcome to the first experiment of the GARRF Virtual Engineering Laboratory. In this experiment, you will explore the relationship between Voltage, Current and Resistance using a virtual circuit.

You will predict the current, run the virtual experiment, observe the result and verify the fundamental relationship:

V = I × R

🚀 A GARRF Virtual Engineering Laboratory — designed for students to learn by building, testing and experimenting.

🎯 Objective

  • Understand the relationship between voltage, current and resistance.
  • Construct a simple virtual electrical circuit.
  • Observe how current changes when resistance changes.
  • Calculate current using Ohm's Law.
  • Compare calculated and observed values.
  • Develop basic laboratory measurement skills.

📚 Theory

Ohm's Law states that, for a resistive electrical circuit, the voltage across a resistor is related to current and resistance by:

V = I R

Therefore:

I = V / R

where V is voltage in volts, I is current in amperes, and R is resistance in ohms.

🤖 CHITTI — Your Virtual Laboratory Mentor

CHITTI is the GARRF robotics and engineering AI mentor. If you are confused about the circuit, wiring, programming or the experiment concept, ask CHITTI for guidance.

Try asking:

  • Why does current decrease when resistance increases?
  • What is Ohm's Law?
  • How should I connect the resistor?
  • Why is my LED not working?
  • What should I record in my observation table?
CHITTI says:

Start by checking the three fundamental quantities: Voltage (V), Current (I), and Resistance (R). Remember: V = I × R. Change only one variable at a time and observe what happens.

🧠 Student Challenge

1. If voltage remains constant and resistance increases, what happens to current?
2. A 5 V supply is connected across a 1 kΩ resistor. What is the current?
3. What happens to current if resistance is reduced while voltage remains constant?
