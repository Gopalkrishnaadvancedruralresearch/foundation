SECTION 1

What are Waves & What is a Lamb Wave?

1. What is a Wave?

A wave is energy moving through a material without shifting the material permanently. Think of throwing a pebble into a silent pond: ripples spread outward carrying kinetic energy, but the water molecules themselves stay in place after moving up and down.

2. What is a Lamb Wave?

When an ultrasonic wave travels through a thin plate (like an aluminum sheet, aircraft skin, or steel girder), it bounces continuously between top and bottom surfaces. This guided elastic wave is called a Lamb Wave.

3. Where Do Lamb Waves Exist?

Lamb waves exist throughout the entire thickness of thin plate structures, travelling long distances to detect internal cracks, surface corrosion, and hidden fatigue defects.

SECTION 2

Understanding Transducers: Actuators vs. Sensors

🔴 What is a PZT Actuator?

An Actuator converts electrical voltage into mechanical wave vibrations using Piezoelectric ceramic wafers (PZT). It acts as the "speaker" injecting Lamb waves into the plate.

🟢 What is a PZT Sensor?

A Sensor catches passing mechanical wave vibrations and converts them back into electrical voltage signals so we can display them on an oscilloscope (A-Scan output).

SECTION 5

How to Find Damage Location Using Time-of-Flight (ToF)

Step A: Record Healthy Baseline Signal

Actuate a clean plate without damage. You will see a single primary wave packet arriving at the Sensor at time t_direct. This is your Healthy Baseline Signal.

Step B: Observe Damaged Signal Behavior

When damage occurs, part of the wave strikes the damage boundary and bounces back. The Sensor catches two distinct pulses:

1. Direct Wave: Arrives along Actuator → Sensor (time: t_direct).
2. Scattered Echo: Arrives along Actuator → Damage → Sensor (time: t_echo).

Step C: Calculate Damage Distance (Principle)

Using wave speed inside aluminum (5.4 km/s or 5400 m/s), calculate total travel distance:

Total Distance = Wave Speed × t_echo

By measuring echo arrival times across multiple sensor paths, the exact (X, Y) crack coordinates are calculated on the plate surface.
