1. Theory & Mathematical Foundations

Piezoelectric Guided Lamb Waves & Structural Health Monitoring (SHM)
Guided waves are elastic stress waves bounded by thin plate boundaries (e.g., metallic or composite skins). Surface-bonded Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT) transducers convert electrical voltage into mechanical strain via the inverse piezoelectric effect to actuate ultrasonic Lamb waves across a structure.

1.1 Lamb Wave Wave Propagation Modes

Lamb waves propagate in two fundamental, dispersive wave modes:

  • Symmetric ($S_0$ Mode): Extensional in-plane motion, dominating high-frequency wave packets with high group velocity.
  • Antisymmetric ($A_0$ Mode): Flexural out-of-plane motion, highly sensitive to surface discontinuities, notch boundaries, and localized corrosion.

1.2 Time-of-Flight (ToF) & Damage Scattering Physics

For a direct wave path between Actuator ($A$) and Sensor ($S$) with distance $d_{AS}$, the direct Time-of-Flight ($ToF_{direct}$) is given by:

ToF_direct = d_AS / c_g

where $c_g$ is the group velocity ($c_g \approx 5.4\text{ km/s}$ for $A_0$ modes in aluminum plates). When a defect ($D$) exists at coordinates $(X_D, Y_D)$, an echo path $A \rightarrow D \rightarrow S$ creates a secondary wave scatter with total distance $d_{scatter} = d_{AD} + d_{DS}$:

ToF_scatter = (d_AD + d_DS) / c_g | Δt = ToF_scatter - ToF_direct

By comparing the **Healthy Baseline Signal** against the **Damaged Signal**, the delay $\Delta t$ pinpoints the exact ellipse on which the damage resides.
