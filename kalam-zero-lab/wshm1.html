3. Experiment Background & Physics

What is a Lamb Wave?

Lamb waves are guided elastic stress waves that propagate through thin, plate-like solid structures bounded by two parallel free surfaces. Unlike simple bulk acoustic waves, Lamb waves are guided by the plate's upper and lower boundaries, causing the entire cross-section of the plate to vibrate as the wave travels.

Two Fundamental Propagation Modes:

  • Symmetric Mode (S₀): In-plane stretching and compression along the plate's neutral axis (accordion motion).
  • Asymmetric Mode (A₀): Out-of-plane flexural and bending motion, creating microscopic surface ripples.

Why is this Virtual Experiment Required?

Piezoelectric Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) allows engineers to evaluate structural integrity without destroying components. This virtual module provides key operational benefits:

  • Visual Intuition: Ultrasonic Lamb waves travel at several kilometers per second. This virtual canvas slows down high-speed wave mechanics for real-time observation.
  • Damage Localization (ToF): Demonstrates how to locate structural defects using Time-of-Flight ($\text{ToF} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Group Velocity}}$) and echo-scattering analysis.
  • Open Educational Access: Replicates expensive physical hardware (arbitrary function generators, high-bandwidth DSOs, and PZT transducers) inside a light, accessible web browser interface.
