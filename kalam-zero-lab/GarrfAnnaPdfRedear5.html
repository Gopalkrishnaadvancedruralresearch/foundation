1. Quick start (3 steps)

  1. Click 📁 Upload PDF / Word and choose one paper – or many papers at once (hold Ctrl/Shift, or select several files).
  2. Click 🎓 Offline Class (UG) or 📚 Offline Class (PG / Faculty), then ▶ Start Class. Listen and watch the board.
  3. When the class ends (or at any moment) type or speak a question. The answer comes from all the papers you uploaded.

Use Chrome, Edge or Safari for voice. Firefox cannot take spoken questions. The first time, open the page with internet so the PDF, Word and OCR helpers load; after that it can work offline from the browser cache.

5. Offline Class – UG, PG / Faculty, and what each does

An Offline Class is a full-screen, teacher-led lesson built from your open PDF. It needs no AI service. It does not skip your paper's text: every readable sentence is on the board, the voice explains it, and the PDF page follows on the right.

🎓 UG (undergraduate)📚 PG / Faculty / Researcher
StylePlain language, slower voice, complex words simplified, abbreviations expanded when first used.Technical wording kept, analytical commentary, assumptions and limitations flagged.
SlidesAbout 5 sentences per slide.About 4 sentences per slide (more slides).
Questions"Can you say the main result in your own words?""What baseline makes this meaningful? Which assumption would you challenge?"
EndingWhole paper in one breath, then a self-test with answers revealed.Big picture, check of understanding, then a critical appraisal (novelty, validity, evidence, reproducibility, generality).
BothWelcome and time estimate · map of the paper · "words you will hear" · a short opener and a recap for each section · key-idea line and think-pause · figures, tables and equations explained with the text that refers to them · links to your other papers when you have several.

"All" slides: the class covers all pages (references are skipped and left in the PDF).

8. What you can ask

  • Definitions: "What is SHM?" · "What does PZT stand for?"
  • Explanations: "How did they prepare the specimens?" · "Why is temperature a problem?"
  • Results and limits: "What did they find?" · "What limitations are mentioned?"
  • Summaries: "Summarize this paper" · "Summarize each paper"
  • Across papers: "Which paper talks about damping?" · "Compare the papers on error"
  • Specific items: "Explain Figure 2" · "Table 1" · "Equation 3"
  • Go deeper: type or say "more".

Answers show source chips (paper and page). Click a chip on the main screen to open that paper at that page.

9. Tips and limits

  • Good results need a text-based PDF. Scanned or badly encoded PDFs are weaker.
  • Some fonts export brackets or Greek letters as boxes. Brackets are repaired where possible; Greek symbols may be missing – check the PDF page.
  • Explanations are produced by rules from the paper's own sentences, not invented facts. They can still be incomplete or mistaken.
  • Headings, figure and table detection can fail on unusual layouts. Use the PDF and the 🔍 Magnifier to check.
  • If you hear nothing: check volume, that the tab is not muted, use 🔈 Test voice, and try Chrome or Edge.
