⚡ INTRODUCTION — Lamb Wave Structural Health Monitoring (SHM)

Guided Lamb waves propagate through thin-walled structures like metallic plates and composite panels. Because of their long propagation range and sensitivity to internal structural flaws, they form the cornerstone of advanced Structural Health Monitoring (SHM).

🎯 Experiment Objective

To excite and sense Lamb wave packets (S0 and A0 modes) using piezoelectric (PZT) wafers, measure time-of-flight, and analyze signal attenuation due to structural damage.

📚 SECTION 1 — Theory of Guided Lamb Waves

Wave Propagation Principles

Lamb waves consist of particle motion constrained between two parallel free surfaces. The governing Rayleigh-Lamb frequency equations define multi-mode propagation:

$$\frac{\tan(\beta h)}{\tan(\alpha h)} = -\left[ \frac{4\alpha\beta k^2}{(k^2 - \beta^2)^2} \right]^{\pm 1}$$

Time of Flight & Distance

The propagation distance $d$ related to group velocity $C_g$ and time of flight $t$ is expressed as:

$d = C_g \times t$

Important Observation

At lower frequency-thickness products, the fundamental antisymmetric mode ($A_0$) and symmetric mode ($S_0$) dominate wave signals, allowing precise defect identification via amplitude drop and wave scattering.
