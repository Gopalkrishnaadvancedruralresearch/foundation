Step Mechanical Instructions

Bench Warning: Verify Motor Zero First!

Before screwing the aluminum horn to the splined shaft, supply 1500µs PWM (90°). If assembled at 0° or 180°, the limb will strike hard mechanical limits and strip internal reduction gears upon boot!

"Why Two Sides on Each Motor?"

Standard humanoid U-brackets sandwich the servo: Side A is the active 25T splined horn driven by the motor, while Side B is an idle chrome flanged ball bearing. This gives true dual-axis structural rigidity without paying for industrial twin-shaft actuators!

Why Can't We Use One Side Like RC Cars?

In an RC car, a servo only steers lightweight steering rods. In a 40cm humanoid robot weighing 1.8 kg, when the robot balances on one leg, 18 Newton-meters of dynamic cantilever torque attempts to snap the output spline sideways!

The Flanged Bearing Solution

By inserting an M3 flanged radial ball bearing on the rear face and tightening a pivot screw into the dummy rear locator hole, the aluminum bracket forms a closed structural box beam. The gear shaft only bears rotational torque, while the bearing absorbs all radial shear forces!
