Theoretical Foundation & Mathematical Formulations

World's First Quantum AI Integrated Metamaterial Electromagnetic Radiator for SHM

1. Historical Precedence: Annamdas & Soh (SPIE 2011/2012) Metamaterial SHM

The landmark adaptation of electromagnetic passive metamaterials for structural health monitoring was pioneered by Dr. Venu Gopal Madhav Annamdas and Prof. Chee Kiong Soh (Nanyang Technological University, Singapore). Documented in their historical paper presented at the SPIE Smart Structures and NDE symposium:

Citation: Annamdas, V. G. M., & Soh, C. K. "Influence of loading on the near field based passive metamaterial in structural health monitoring." In SPIE Smart Structures and Materials + Nondestructive Evaluation and Health Monitoring (2011/2012).

Annamdas and Soh demonstrated that a passive circular metamaterial unit cell (resonating in the 3 to 8 GHz microwave spectrum, with a characteristic localized surface plasmon (LSP) dip near 6.035 GHz) is perturbed by mechanical strain and deflection in the host structural beam. The mechanical strain shifts the local permittivity $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ and permeability $\mu_{\text{eff}}$, causing a measurable shift in the transmission notch $\Delta f_0$ and magnitude in both direct bonded (contact) and standoff (non-contact) near-field evanescent configurations.

2. Localized Surface Plasmon (LSP) Coupling & Frequency Shift

The metamaterial resonator's equivalent $RLC$ circuit resonance frequency is governed by the effective inductance $L_{\text{eff}}$ of the metallic ring and the localized capacitance $C_{\text{eff}}$ established between the metamaterial resonator and the host specimen surface:

$$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L_{\text{eff}} \cdot C_{\text{eff}}(\sigma, d_{\text{gap}})}}$$

When an external mechanical stress $\sigma_{xx}$ is applied, the host material undergoes elastic strain $\varepsilon_{xx} = \sigma_{xx}/E$. Through the piezo-dielectric or photoelastic effect, the effective dielectric permittivity of the substrate alters:

$$\Delta \varepsilon_{\text{eff}} = \chi_m \cdot \sigma_{xx} - \beta \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{d_{\text{gap}}}{\delta_p}\right)$$

where $\delta_p = c / (2\pi \sqrt{f_0^2 (\varepsilon_r \mu_r - 1)})$ is the evanescent penetration depth, and $\chi_m$ is the stress-dielectric coupling tensor.

3. Quantitative Damage Metric: Annamdas & Soh RMSD Formulation

To quantify damage severity independent of absolute signal scale, the Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) metric computes variance between the pristine baseline spectrum $S_{21}^{\text{baseline}}(f_i)$ and the damaged/loaded spectrum $S_{21}^{\text{damaged}}(f_i)$ over $N$ discrete frequency points:

$$\text{RMSD} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \left[\text{Re}\left(S_{21}^{\text{damaged}}(f_i)\right) - \text{Re}\left(S_{21}^{\text{baseline}}(f_i)\right)\right]^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N \left[\text{Re}\left(S_{21}^{\text{baseline}}(f_i)\right)\right]^2}} \times 100\%$$

An RMSD index between $0 - 2\%$ indicates healthy/pristine baseline state; $2 - 10\%$ corresponds to micro-crack/stress concentration; and values exceeding $15\%$ indicate critical structural cracks requiring immediate remediation.

4. Sub-THz G-Band (110–300 GHz) Photoelasticity & Flaw Scattering

In the Sub-THz G-band ($\lambda_0 \approx 1.0 - 2.7\text{ mm}$), electromagnetic waves exhibit sub-millimeter penetration through composite and oxide layers. Stress-induced birefringence produces an anisotropic index of refraction:

$$n_x - n_y = C_{\text{photo}} \cdot (\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy})$$

where $C_{\text{photo}}$ is the photoelastic constant. Micro-cracks with dimensions $a \ll \lambda$ act as Rayleigh scatterers with differential radar cross section (RCS):

$$\sigma_{\text{RCS}} = \frac{8\pi^3 a^6}{\lambda_0^4} \left|\frac{\varepsilon_r - 1}{\varepsilon_r + 2}\right|^2 \cdot \cos^2(\theta_{\text{incident}})$$
5. Quantum AI (QNN/VQE) Inversion Engine Formulation

Conventional classical inverse scattering formulations for non-destructive evaluation (NDE) suffer from severe exponential scaling when handling multiscale defects and non-linear electromagnetic wavefields. Our Quantum Neural Network (QNN) encodes the measured $S$-parameter scatter vector $\vec{S} \in \mathbb{R}^M$ into an $n$-qubit quantum register:

$$|\psi_{\text{in}}\rangle = \bigotimes_{j=1}^n \left[\cos\left(\frac{S_j \pi}{2}\right)|0\rangle + \sin\left(\frac{S_j \pi}{2}\right)|1\rangle\right]$$

A Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) ansatz $U(\vec{\theta}) = \prod_{l=1}^L \left( W_{\text{entangle}} \prod_{k=1}^n R_y(\theta_{l,k}) \right)$ transforms the state across $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space. The structural stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ and defect coordinates $\vec{r}_{\text{crack}}$ are evaluated as the expectation value of Pauli observable operators:

$$\hat{\sigma}_{xx} = \langle \psi(\vec{\theta}) | \hat{Z}_0 \otimes \hat{Z}_1 | \psi(\vec{\theta}) \rangle, \quad \mathcal{L}(\vec{\theta}) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_k \left| \langle \hat{O}_k \rangle_{\vec{\theta}} - \sigma_k^{\text{true}} \right|^2$$

Variational parameter updates follow the analytic quantum parameter-shift rule:

$$\frac{\partial \langle \hat{O} \rangle}{\partial \theta_i} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \langle \hat{O} \rangle_{\theta_i + \pi/2} - \langle \hat{O} \rangle_{\theta_i - \pi/2} \right]$$
