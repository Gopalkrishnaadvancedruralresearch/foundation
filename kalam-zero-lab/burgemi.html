Explore how Electromechanical Impedance (EMI) can be taught using a 100-section tower model. Select a section, inspect its ten virtual PZT patches, compare baseline conductance and susceptance with a simulated second measurement, and explore how a hypothetical local change may alter the signature.

Educational digital twin — for simulation and learning only. This model is designed to help students explore structural health monitoring concepts, compare illustrative baseline and changed EMI signatures, and learn how monitoring information may support timely inspection and early repair planning. It is not intended to predict, represent, or diagnose the real-time or actual condition of the Burj Khalifa or any real structure. All tower sections, PZT locations, sensor readings, impedance curves, and simulated damage responses are illustrative synthetic data—not measurements from the building. Real-world decisions require validated measurements, appropriate calibration and environmental compensation, and assessment by qualified structural professionals.

How students can use this lab

Suggested workflow
  1. Select a section: note the illustrative level, elevation range, and its ten sensor IDs.
  2. Record each baseline: compare several PZTs first. Healthy sensors can have different resonance frequencies and peak amplitudes.
  3. Choose a local scenario: set crack, degradation, interface loosening, or a healthy repeat. The selected PZT is most affected in this model; neighbouring sensors show weaker changes.
  4. Change severity: repeat at 10%, 35%, 70%, and 100%. The simulated effect generally grows, but the curve is not a universal damage fingerprint.
  5. Compare sensors: separate local changes from common-mode variation and measurement noise before drawing conclusions.
  6. Discuss uncertainty: test whether noise, temperature, bonding quality, or sensor variability could mimic a damage-related change.
