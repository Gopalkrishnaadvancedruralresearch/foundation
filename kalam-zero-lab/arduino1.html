11 🧠 Why?

Why a motor driver?

The Arduino creates control signals. The motor driver handles the higher-current motor power path.

Why a resistor?

A resistor can limit current, create a voltage divider, or provide a pull-up/pull-down depending on its circuit function.

Why a regulator?

The battery voltage may not equal the voltage required by the controller and sensors. Regulation creates a suitable supply rail.

Why feedback?

A robot becomes more controllable when it measures what actually happened rather than assuming the commanded action occurred.

12 🧪 Student Challenges

Challenge 1 — GPIO

Why shouldn't a DC motor normally be powered directly from an Arduino GPIO pin?

The motor may require more current than the GPIO should supply and can produce electrical disturbances. Use an appropriate driver/power stage.
Challenge 2 — Battery

The robot has the correct voltage but still shuts down when both motors start. What might be wrong?

Startup/stall current may cause a voltage drop or exceed the battery, wiring or driver capability.
Challenge 3 — Sensors

A robot detects an obstacle but doesn't know which direction to turn. What could improve the design?

Additional sensing from different positions or directions can provide more information. This is a step toward sensor fusion.
Challenge 4 — AI

What changes when a robot stops following fixed rules and begins learning patterns from data?

The system moves toward machine-learning-based perception or decision making. The student must now think about data, features, models, validation and uncertainty.
