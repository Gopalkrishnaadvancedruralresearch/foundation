This laboratory is designed as a progressive engineering journey. A student can begin with basic DC circuits and digital logic, move into Arduino and ESP32 programming, and then progress toward sensors, motors, autonomous robots, robotic arms, humanoid control, quadruped/dog robots, machine vision and intelligent robotic systems.

The objective is to connect theory → circuit → code → control → sensing → robotics → intelligence.

🚀 Enter the Virtual Engineering Laboratory

Choose an experiment, select a controller and enter the interactive simulation environment. Students can build circuits, program controllers, operate motors and investigate sensors. For robotics experiments, the GARRF curriculum can progressively move from a single servo to robotic arms, mobile robots, humanoids and quadruped/dog robot control.

👨‍🎓 How Students Use GARRF Lab

  1. Choose one experiment from the 50-experiment catalogue.
  2. Read the objective and engineering principle.
  3. Select Arduino, ESP32, Pico or another suitable controller.
  4. Build the virtual circuit or robot controller.
  5. Write or modify the program.
  6. Compile and run.
  7. Observe sensors, motors, displays and signals.
  8. Record measurements and answer questions.
  9. Modify the design and investigate what happens.

🤖 Robotics Progression

The robotics sequence is deliberately progressive:

  • Level 1: Motor and servo control
  • Level 2: Two-wheel mobile robot
  • Level 3: Line following and obstacle avoidance
  • Level 4: IMU, encoders and PID
  • Level 5: Robotic arm and inverse kinematics
  • Level 6: Humanoid upper-body control
  • Level 7: Biped walking
  • Level 8: Quadruped / robot dog gait
  • Level 9: Sensor fusion
  • Level 10: AI autonomous robot
GARRF Forward-Looking Laboratory: This is intentionally designed as a growing master catalogue. The 50 experiments are the initial foundation; additional boards, sensors, robot platforms, AI modules and advanced engineering experiments can be added without changing the overall laboratory structure.
