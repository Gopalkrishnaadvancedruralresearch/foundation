🔬 Experiment 24 — Active Band-Pass Filter

A band-pass filter combines the fundamental ideas of the high-pass filter and low-pass filter. Instead of passing everything above or below one frequency, it allows a selected range of frequencies to pass.

This is an important step toward real engineering signal-processing systems because many sensors do not require the complete frequency spectrum. Engineers often want to isolate the frequency range that contains useful information.

📚 1. Theory — What Is a Band-Pass Filter?

A band-pass filter allows a selected range of frequencies to pass while attenuating frequencies below and above that range.

It therefore has two important cutoff frequencies:

  • Lower cutoff frequency — fL
  • Upper cutoff frequency — fH
Bandwidth = fH − fL
Centre Frequency = √(fL × fH)
Q = Centre Frequency / Bandwidth

A higher Q means a narrower and more selective passband. A lower Q means a wider passband.

Engineering Concept

A practical band-pass filter can be constructed by combining high-pass and low-pass sections:

Input → High-Pass → Low-Pass → Amplifier → Output

The high-pass section establishes the lower cutoff. The low-pass section establishes the upper cutoff.

|HBP| = |HHP| × |HLP|
Aout = Ain × Gain × |HBP|

📡 13. PZT & Structural Health Monitoring

Why is band-pass filtering important for SHM?

Structural systems can generate vibration over a wide range of frequencies. However, a particular structural mode, resonance or damage-sensitive feature may occupy only a limited frequency region.

A band-pass filter can isolate that region and reduce irrelevant components outside the target band.

PZT → Amplifier → High-Pass → Low-Pass → ADC → AI/ML → SHM

For example, if an engineering investigation is interested primarily in a vibration band around a particular structural resonance, filtering can reduce unwanted low-frequency drift and high-frequency noise before feature extraction.

Important Engineering Principle

Filtering must be designed from the physics of the measurement. An incorrectly selected passband can remove information that is actually important for detecting damage.
